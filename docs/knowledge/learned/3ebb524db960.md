When an expression's DSL type cannot be derived by the body typer, the generator should fail with a diagnostic, not emit a runtime-checked form. This is enforced by validators like BODY_TYPE007 which reject storing `/` results into integer slots.

The division semantics of '/' produce exact quotients, never truncating to integers. Integer operands divide into Decimal results, and storing such results in integer slots triggers BODY_TYPE007 errors.

datrix-language/docs/reference/datrix-validators.md:91| - **Division.** `/` is the exact quotient on every target: two integers divide into a `Decimal` (`7 / 2` is `3.5`; body resolution records it on the node as `BinaryOpNode.integer_quotient`), and a `Float` or `Decimal` operand keeps its own family. A quotient is therefore never a whole number, and **`BODY_TYPE007`** refuses one stored into an integer slot — a `let`, an `=` assignment, a `return`, a call argument or a literal's field whose declared type is `Int`, `Long` or `Short`, and every `x /= y` on such a slot — when the value is the `/` itself, arithmetic or a negation over one, or a conditional arm holding one.
